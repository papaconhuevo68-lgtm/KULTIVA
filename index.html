From orbit to fair contracts

The solution in a single chain

Six connected steps where satellite data becomes healthy soil and secure income.

Select a step in the diagram or a card to explore the chain.

01 · Virtual sensor

The satellite is the sensor

Public Earth-observation products can provide rainfall, temperature, soil-moisture, and vegetation indicators; this prototype does not yet ingest or validate those data for a plot.

02 · Diagnosis

Data becomes risk

We detect frost, drought, accumulated heat (harvest), and conditions favorable for blight, moths, or mildew.

03 · Communication

Risk becomes an alert

A future alert could be delivered by WhatsApp or voice note in Spanish; this demo is not connected to a live forecast.

04 · Action

The alert leads to a decision

Clear, jargon-free recommendation: harvest now or wait, and which crop to rotate in the next season.

05 · Regeneration

The decision improves the soil

Rotations with legumes (tarwi or broad bean) can contribute to soil fertility and pest management; they do not by themselves establish organic certification.

06 · Regional scale

Guides the season

Cooperatives and the Regional Agriculture Directorate coordinate planting to meet volumes and organic certification requirements.

adaptation path

Users and adaptation path

We start with those who already have technicians, scale, and need. The farmer receives the recommendation without needing to learn how to use a satellite map.

Level 1 · Main channel

Cooperatives and the Regional Government (DRA)

They can plan the season, record rotation, compare projected supply with demand, and track certification requirements.

Level 2 · Training

Technicians and community leaders

They use visual views in workshops (like the Vidas project) to explain why rotation is needed using data from their own area.

Level 3 · End user

The farmer

Zero setup; receives a clear card in Spanish and Quechua explaining what to plant, when, and why.

Future vision (outside the demo scope): buyers, investors, and rural banks.

What we do

  • • The current page is a prototype; it does not calculate live climate or pest risk.
  • • Spatial resolution belongs to a particular product and band; it is not the accuracy of a farm recommendation.
  • • Each item should be labeled Observed, Classified, Estimated, or Demonstration.

What we do not promise

  • • We do not provide a validated forecast or an exact prediction.
  • • We do not identify pests from space; field scouting is required.
  • • We do not set or verify market prices.

How risk would be assessed

Frost screening needs a dated local minimum-temperature forecast, crop and growth stage, and a crop-specific injury threshold; duration below that threshold and field elevation also matter. This page has no such inputs, so its frost alert is illustrative and no protection action is prescribed.

Erosion is not inferred from elevation alone. A potential soil-loss estimate such as USLE/RUSLE requires rainfall erosivity (R), soil erodibility (K), slope-length/steepness (LS), cover/management (C), and support practice (P), with locally suitable inputs. None are populated here; erosion risk is not calculated.

Evidence status in this demo

  • • Observed: a measurement or field record with source, location, and date.
  • • Classified: a category assigned by a documented method to observations.
  • • Estimated: a model output with inputs, version, and period stated.
  • • Demonstration: illustrative interface content, not a measured result.
